How the estimates are built, and how to check them
  1. Anchors. Each product has dated anchor points: monthly active users (all uses) for a given month, taken from a cited figure or estimated from one. The Estimate anchors tab lists all of them, each with its basis, its sources and the conversion used.
  2. Unit conversion. Figures are converted to monthly active users. Weekly actives are multiplied by 1.35 and daily actives by about 3. Survey shares are multiplied by the professional developer population (28.3M in 2025, range 20M-36.5M). Paid seats are treated as a floor, lifetime installs and downloads as a ceiling, and company-claimed "monthly developers" are discounted by 0.75 where noted.
  3. Monthly series. Between anchors, values are interpolated geometrically, which means a constant monthly growth rate. Each product's uncertainty band is its central path times a product-specific low and high factor (see the Products tab).
  4. Business restriction. Each product's users are multiplied by a business-use share. This share is 50-100% for coding tools; for desktop chat apps it varies by product and year (assumptions B09-B13).
  5. De-duplication. Most people use several tools, so product totals are not added up. Developers are counted top-down: the professional developer population times the share using at least one AI coding tool for work. That share follows survey anchors and a logistic curve from 2024 (B01-B07). Non-developers are counted bottom-up: business desktop-app users, minus the developer share, divided by 1.15 apps per person (B08-B12). The ratio of business installs to de-duplicated developer users (B14) is checked against survey data on how many tools people use.

How to verify a figure

A source Open the row in Sources. It shows the claim taken and every anchor or assumption it feeds. If a page will not load, use its archive link.
A product's number Filter Estimate anchors by product. Each row shows the value, the wording it rests on and the conversion rule.
Own data pulls npm downloads (S80) and VS Code installs (S81) list the exact API query or marketplace page behind each figure, so you can reproduce it.
Source quality A primary: a company statement, filing, survey or API. B a news report of a primary claim. C an aggregator, blog or wiki, used only where nothing better exists.
