grh-lean: a different Lean proof of the Riemann hypothesis, in 300,000 lines of code instead of 15.5 million

On 20 November 2026 Levent Alpöge published a machine-checked proof of the Riemann hypothesis and GRH for Dirichlet L-functions, generated with Claude: 15.5 million lines of Lean code. On 25 November grh-lean proved the same five theorems in 300,000 lines of code. The evidence points to a substantially different proof rather than a trimmed copy. How it was produced has not been disclosed. All counts on this page are lines of code, excluding comments and blank lines.

A different proof, not a pruned one

Removing everything the original never used, plus code that only feeds its placeholder objects, cuts it by 23–31%. The remaining 40× reduction comes from replacing the core of the argument.

Lines of code

The pruning figure is measured on the original's full kernel export (1,468,418 declarations), walked from the five theorems. The range comes from names that could not be matched with certainty.

  • The core changedNo Ferrero–Washington, no dependence on Vandiver's conjecture, no abelian varieties. Abelian variety theory was 7.3M lines of code, 47% of the original. No file in grh-lean mentions it.
  • Lemmas were rewritten, not carried overOnly 8–14% of declaration names occur in the original, and matching names often state different theorems.
  • What stayedThe five theorem statements (byte-identical) and the top-level strategy. The authors say the rewrite "follows that proof's method; many steps are proved by other arguments".
  • Placeholder objects are goneThe original contains definitions whose fields are trivially True or zero (geoDegenerate, anyGeometry). These are the objects J. L. Nielsen's critique of the original refers to. Neither grh-lean edition contains them or anything of that kind.
  • OpenWhether the new route was found by learning from the original, by avoiding its weak points, or independently is not stated.

300,000 lines of code is the number that matters

The readable edition is the version a person could work through. It has 299,902 lines of code; its 138,466 comment lines and 38,756 blank lines come on top, for 477,124 lines in total. The compact edition is the same argument compressed to 76,151 lines of code: inlined lemmas, heavy automation, 72-character lines. It is smaller, but harder to read. The original has no comments: its 17.4 million total lines are 15.5 million lines of code plus 1.8 million blank lines. No mathematician has read either edition through.

What has not been disclosed

The repository is a single commit with a README. Nothing about how the proof was produced has been published.

Verified by machine, understood by no one

Andrew Sutherland (MIT) checked the original and both editions; Michael Rubinstein (Waterloo) checked the original and the compact edition. Every check passed. Each proof was built from source and re-checked by six independently written proof checkers. Its statements were matched against an independently written challenge file, and it uses only Lean's three standard axioms. These checks establish that the theorems are proved. They do not establish that anyone understands the proof.
